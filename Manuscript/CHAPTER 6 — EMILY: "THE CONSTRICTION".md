CHAPTER 6 — EMILY: "THE CONSTRICTION"

The gray light didn’t bring relief; it just made the shadows in the cabin thin and sharp.
The chorus of voices had vanished with the fog, leaving behind a silence so dense it made Emily’s eardrums ache. She was still on the floor, her knees locked over Mara’s shins, her muscles stiff and burning from hours of forced immobility.
Beneath her, Mara had stopped fighting. The girl lay entirely rigid, her eyes rolled back into her head so that only the bloodshot whites showed, her jaw clamped shut in a hard, catatonic lock. Her limbs weren't relaxed; they were frozen like dry pine.
Alex slid backward on his shins, his hand dropping to the floorboards to stabilize himself. He didn't stand up. He didn't make a sound.
Then, the nature of the quiet changed.
Click.
It wasn't outside. It was directly above them. A sharp, structural snap that vibrated through the moss-slicked roof shingles.
Emily held her breath, her heart slamming against her ribs so violently she was terrified the noise would pass through her chest. She looked at Alex. He was staring at the back door seam, his face cast in a sickly, gray pallor.
They didn't see the glass break. There was no explosion of timber.
Instead, the horror came from how silently the wood failed. Through the half-inch gap where the back door had warped away from the frame, a long, dark appendage began to slide.
What the freak is that?
The thought hit Emily’s brain like a cold spike of adrenaline, short-circuiting her lungs. It wasn't a hand. It was an anomaly of bone and wet slate, splitting into five multi-jointed, elongated fingers that extended at least a foot past the knuckles, each one ending in a needle-sharp, yellowed claw. It moved with a sickening, liquid fluidness, completely blind, splaying out into the damp air of the kitchen corner.
The claws hovered. They didn't strike. They drifted back and forth, vibrating slightly, listening to the room.
Then, Emily’s boot heel shifted a fraction of an inch against a loose floorboard. A tiny, dry creak cut through the room.
The response was instantaneous.
The long fingers snapped toward the sound, locking onto the iron latch of the back door from the inside. With terrifying, blind precision, the claws lifted the bar. The latch cleared the slot with a sharp clack, and the door swung inward, throwing a wall of freezing gray fog into the cabin.
"Mara," Emily breathed, the whisper slipping from her lips before she could stop it.
The silhouette filled the opening. It was towering—at least seven feet tall—its lean, emaciated torso cutting off the morning light. It had no eyes, no nose, just a smooth, pale expanse of skin that split horizontally across the middle of the head into a vast, unhinging maw lined with needle-thick teeth.
Oh my god. Oh my god, what is that thing? Total, unadulterated primal terror flooded Emily's chest, freezing her blood. It wasn't a man. It wasn't an animal. It was a nightmare pulled out of the dark.
The moment her whisper hit the air, the creature's head snapped toward them.
Click.
Mara’s breath finally broke in a sharp, terrified gasp.
The entity lunged, propelling itself across the small kitchen aisle with an unnatural, blurring speed.
Before it could reach the center of the room, Alex surged upward. With a guttural groan of pure exertion, he slammed his full weight sideways into the unhinged cast-iron cookstove, shoving the massive block of iron directly into the creature's path.
The entity collided violently with the iron obstacle. The stove hit the floorboards with a deafening, floor-shaking crash, throwing up a cloud of soot and broken pipe. The creature stumbled, its long arms snapping outward to catch its balance against the log walls, its blind head whipping back and forth as it scrambled to clear the obstruction.
"Go!" Alex roared, his voice cracking with panic. "Emily, pick her up and go!"
The creature’s head snapped instantly toward the sound of his roar, the massive, unhinging maw turning entirely away from Emily and locking onto Alex.
Emily didn't wait. She couldn't think. She scooped her arms beneath Mara’s rigid knees and shoulders, hoisting the twelve-year-old’s dead weight against her chest. Her muscles screamed under the sudden strain as she staggered backward, her boots skidding on the grit, throwing her shoulder against the front door latch.
She kicked the timber open.
The freezing morning fog hit her face. She stumbled out onto the warped porch steps, her lungs burning, the weight of the catatonic girl dragging her down toward the mud.
Behind her, inside the cabin, a horrific, tearing sound echoed through the threshold—the unmistakable screech of wood and fabric fracturing under a heavy strike.
"Alex!" she shrieked into the gray mist, but there was no reply.
From the trees surrounding the clearing, the response began.
Click.
Click. Click. Click-click.
The noise erupted from the fog in a rapid, multi-directional crossfire. The entities in the woods had heard the noise of the crash. They were already moving, closing the semicircle around the porch, mapping the distance through the gray veil.
Emily didn't look back. She couldn't risk what she might see in the doorway. Tightening her arms around Mara’s frozen body, she plunged directly off the steps and into the suffocating, freezing morning fog of the valley.
She ran blindly, her sneakers tearing through the wet mud in absolute, terrified silence, the icy air scraping her throat. Tears of pure panic blurred her vision as she threw herself into the thick undergrowth at the edge of the trees, branches clawing at her face.
She kept moving until her legs threatened to collapse beneath her, finally sinking into the deep shadow of a fallen birch. Panting, her chest heaving in short, silent gasps, she clutched Mara against her collarbone and looked back toward the gray void she had just escaped.
The fog was a solid wall of white.
There were no footsteps behind her. No sound of boots crushing the leaves.
Only the distant, muffled rhythm of clicking echoing through the high canopy.
Emily pulled Mara closer into the dark hollow of the roots, her heart shattering against her ribs as she stared into the mist. She was completely alone in the freezing woods with a catatonic child, and she had no idea if Alex was even alive.
