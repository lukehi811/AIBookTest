CHAPTER 4 — EMILY: "SHARED SHELTER"
The floorboards beneath their backs had just begun to absorb the shared warmth of their bodies when the sound cut through the cabin.
Thump. Thump-thump.
It was low, wet, and frantic. Not the heavy, aggressive striking of an intruder trying to splinter the timber, but the desperate, uncoordinated pounding of small palms against the lower half of the door. It sounded brief, weak, and rapidly losing strength.
Alex moved before the sound could even finish its rhythm.
Despite the dull, agonizing catch in his lower left ribs, he surged upward from the dresser, his boots making no sound against the grit as his hand dropped into the pocket of his canvas jacket, his fingers clamping around the heavy handle of his pocket knife. He stood completely rigid beside the doorframe, his weight shifted forward, his chin tucked down as he listened to the wood.
Emily was right behind him, the oversized sleeves of her borrowed wool sweater bunching at her elbows as she grabbed the rusted iron poker from the floor. Her heart was slamming against her ribs, the terrifying memory of Tommy’s cut-off shriek from an hour earlier flooding back into her throat. A sharp, wet, agonizing sound—a throat-shredding shriek of pure physical terror.
From the other side of the thick timber came a sob.
It was high, fractured, and unmistakably young. "Please," a ragged voice gasped, the sound muffled by the rain and the wood. "Please open it. I know someone's in there. I saw the door. Please."
Alex’s jaw hardened. He didn't reach for the latch. His eyes darted to the small, distorted windowpane, scanning the gray fog of the clearing behind the porch. He didn't look like a man deciding whether to be kind; he looked like a soldier analyzing an ambush. "Em," he muttered under his breath, his voice barely a vibration in the room. "Stay back from the seam."
"Alex, it's a kid," Emily whispered fiercely. Her protective empathy, the deep-seated reflex that made it impossible for her to turn away from someone else’s terror, pulled her forward. She could hear the distinct, rhythmic clicking of the girl's teeth through the door—the exact same violent, freezing tremor that had racked her own jaw just hours before. "Listen to her. She's freezing."
"The boy heard his mother," Alex said, his voice terrifyingly steady, his eyes never leaving the glass pane. "We don't know."
"Please!" the voice outside rose into a frantic, breaking shriek as a heavy gust of wind rattled the moss-slicked roof shingles. "There's something out here! They... they were taken! Open the door!"
The sheer panic in the girl’s voice wasn't a recording. It had the jagged, unpolished edge of total psychological collapse.
Emily didn't wait for Alex's tactical approval. She stepped past him, her hand reaching straight for the heavy iron latch. Alex made a sharp, warning motion with his forearm, but he didn't grab her wrist; he simply stepped closer to the opening, bracing his shoulder against the log wall, his pocket knife clicking open in the shadow of his thigh.
Emily threw the latch up and pulled the heavy door inward three inches.
The wet, freezing air hit them like a physical blow, carrying the scent of rotten pine needles and copper. Crouched on the warped, lichen-green steps of the porch was a girl.
She couldn't have been older than twelve or thirteen. She wore a yellow school sweater that was completely black with mud along the sleeves, her small knees tucked tightly against her chest as she tried to make herself invisible under the gray sky. Her hair was plaster-flat against her forehead from the rain, and her eyes were wide, bloodshot circles of absolute, animal terror.
The moment the door cracked, the girl lunged forward, not checking for danger, simply desperate to escape the open air.
Emily caught her by the shoulders, her hands sinking into the cold, saturated wool of the girl's sweater, and hauled her across the threshold. Alex instantly slammed his weight against the heavy timber door, shoving the iron latch back down into its slot with a definitive, heavy clack that sealed the room back into its shadows.
The girl collapsed onto the floorboards, her knees giving out completely as she slid into the grit. She didn't speak. She lay on her side, her chest heaving in short, shallow, hyperventilating gasps that sounded like an engine running out of oil. Her entire body was vibrating, a violent, uncontrollable shivering that made her limbs twitch against the wood.
"Hey, hey. Look at me," Emily murmured, dropping to her knees beside her. She instantly began peeling off the massive, dry wool sweater she had borrowed from the dresser, leaving herself in just her thin shirt as she wrapped the heavy, dry fabric around the girl's shoulders. "You're inside. You're dry. It's okay."
Alex stood over them, his eyes tracking the mud trailing from the girl's sneakers, then shifting to the closed door, listening for any sound of a pursuit. His knife remained open, held tight against his leg. "What's your name?" he asked. His voice wasn't cruel, but it had a sharp, demanding clarity that cut through the small room.
The girl didn't look at him. She stared blindly at the rusted iron stove, her fingers clutching the edges of Emily's dry sweater so hard her knuckles turned a stark, bloodless white. "M-Mara," she choked out, her teeth clicking violently against each other. "Mara."
"Are you alone, Mara?" Emily asked softly, her hands gently pressing against the girl's trembling shoulders to ground her. "Did you wake up out there?"
Mara nodded frantically, a strand of wet hair whipping across her cheek. "In the dirt. Near... near some big rocks. My watch... my watch wouldn't move."
Alex shifted his weight, his eyes darkening as he looked down at the girl. The amnesia, his mind noted instantly. The timeline gap. It's the same. He knelt down slowly, favoring his left ribs, his expression serious but dropping the initial defensive edge. "Did you see anyone else out there? Anyone before you found the cabin?"
The moment the question left his mouth, Mara’s entire body went completely rigid.
The frantic shivering stopped, replaced by a sudden, terrifying stasis. Her chest locked mid-breath. Her eyes, dilated and dark, fixed onto the space between Alex and Emily with a hollow, unblinking intensity that looked entirely unnatural for a child.
"Mara?" Emily whispered, her heart squeezing with a sudden, protective dread. "It's okay. You can tell us."
"I found them," Mara whispered. Her voice had dropped all its frantic volume, turning into a flat, dry monotone that seemed to freeze the air in the cabin. "Two of them. A man and a lady. They were... they were by a fallen tree. They told me to hide."
"Where are they now?" Alex asked, his eyes narrowing.
Mara didn't turn her head. A single, heavy tear tracked through the dark mud on her cheek, but her expression didn't change. It remained an absolute mask of shock. "They're gone."
"Did they run?" Emily pressed gently.
"No," Mara whispered. Her fingers tightened on the wool until the fabric groaned. She leaned forward slightly, her breath coming in a tiny, cold plume that hit Emily’s shoulder. "They didn't run. The trees... the trees started clicking. And then they were just... taken. There were screams. I heard them screaming. But then it stopped. Everything stopped. I ran. I just ran until I saw the roof."
Mara choked, a sharp, wet gasp rattling in her throat. She clamped her jaws shut so hard a tiny bead of bright red appeared where her teeth bit into her lower lip. She shook her head violently, pulling the oversized sweater over her face, refusing to say another word.
Emily field-dressed the child's panic by pulling her into her arms, wrapping her hands around the back of Mara's wet head, holding her tight against her chest as the child began to weep—a silent, shoulder-shaking sob that carried no sound into the room.
Emily looked up at Alex over Mara's head.
The confirmation was absolute. The freshman boy, Tommy, hadn't been an isolated attack. Whatever force occupied this valley was methodically executing anyone who wandered into the open spaces between the trunks, waiting for them in the dark, and removing the evidence.
Alex stood up slowly, his face grim. He walked back to the window, his boots clicking faint against the floor. He didn't open his knife any further, but he didn't close it either. He stood by the glass, looking out into the gray, suffocating mist that was beginning to swallow the perimeter of the clearing.
The rain continued its dull, rhythmic tapping.
Then, the nature of the quiet changed.
It didn't start with a crash. It started with a vibration. Far out in the deep woods, past the dense line of birches where Emily had watched the freshman boy vanish, a sound began to rise.
"...Mara?..."
The voice was faint, carrying over the patter of the water against the roof. It was distorted by the distance, muffled by the heavy air, but it had a distinct, older quality—a man’s voice, deep and resonant.
"...Mara, sweetie, we're by the rocks... Come back outside... It's safe..."
Inside the cabin, Mara went entirely still in Emily’s embrace. Her head lifted slowly, her eyes filling with a wild, desperate confusion that broke through the shock. "That's... that's Mr. Davis," she whispered, her voice cracking. "The man from the tree. He's... he's alive?"
Alex didn't look back from the window. His hand reached out, his fingers catching the wooden frame of the glass, pressing until the wood groaned under his weight. "Don't move," he said. His tone was an absolute command, stripping any trace of doubt from the room.
The voice from the forest grew clearer, shifting its cadence, the words carrying an unnerving, perfect clarity through the gray downpour.
"...Mara... over here... further is safer... come to the tree..."
And beneath the words, so low it was felt in the floorboards rather than heard in the ears, began the rhythm.
Click.
A dry, structural snap from the edge of the clearing.
Click. Click-click.
"But he's calling me," Mara said, her legs twitching beneath the heavy wool as if the sound were a physical wire pulling her toward the latch. "He told me to hide, he's—"
"Mara, stop. Look at me," Emily said, her hands locking onto the girl’s jaw, forcing the child's face around. Emily’s own voice was shaking, stripped of any comfort, leaving only a hard, desperate panic. "The boy outside... Tommy. He heard his mother. He ran into the trees because he thought she was real, and he... he didn't come back."
Mara’s eyes widened, a tear spilling over her mud-streaked cheek. "But Mr. Davis is—"
"We don't know who is speaking," Emily cut her off, her fingers tightening slightly against the girl's skin to stop the violent clicking of her teeth. "We don't know what's out there. I don't care if it sounds like him. I don't care if it sounds like my own mother. We are not moving. We don't trust anything out there right now. Do you hear me? Nothing."
Alex didn't look back from the window. His hand reached out, his fingers catching the wooden frame of the glass, pressing until the old pine groaned under his weight. His pocket knife remained open in his right hand, the small blade catching the dull gray light.
"She's right," he said, his voice flat, low, and final. "We stay behind the timber."
